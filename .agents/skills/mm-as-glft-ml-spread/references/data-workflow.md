# Data workflow

Read [dataset_scope.json](../../../../data/dataset_scope.json) for the version's calendar, markets, source restrictions, accounting exceptions and migration status, then the bound source/quality manifests. The [data guide](../../../../data/README.md) owns interfaces; verify actual CLI help before use.

Use quality tied to the current files and parser. Historical `data_quality.py` exclusions or old supplier bad-day reports do not admit/exclude new inputs; consult them only for matching historical identities. Keep declared dates, unknown support and previous-use status. A known exchange interruption is not a recurring download obligation: retain measured gaps/staleness and unsupported outcomes.

Preserve purchased originals and separate derivatives. Honor the actual source policy; retained alternate downloaders do not grant fallback. Match venue/contract/symbol/units/channel, never silently substitute reference or spot markets.

Restore contiguous complete messages before atomic snapshots; no non-contiguous grouping or partial snapshot publication. Retain all known depth before Top-N and same-version BBO. Rebase is not market cancel flow or free queue reset. Missing native sequence/order/queue fields remain unavailable.

Separate source clock meaning, output time, observation age and modeled readiness. Provider receipt is provenance/grouping, not local strategy time or network calibration. Unit conversion does not prove clock origin. Carry only past valid state, retaining observation age; no future-fill or sampling-driven freshness.

Deduplicate by reliable market/trade identity and equal content; conflicting IDs fail, distinct IDs at one timestamp survive. Individual events, native packets and derived groups differ. Unknown quantities/counts cannot become zero; observed-empty is not proof of exchange-zero. Carried valuation prices are not new trades/OHLC.

Test atomic messages, chunk/shard invariance, cross-day context, conservation, identity, missing/stale and future visibility. Whole-calendar acceptance needs actual daily results; small fixtures are not full acceptance. Rebuild affected derivatives after source/schema changes and reject old-artifact fallback.

This is the authoritative transfer and working-cache lifecycle policy. Remove disposable working copies immediately after the last actual consumer is fully accepted and exited and retained copies, queued/recovery dependencies and open files are checked. Do not wait 14 days or for the whole experiment. Historical unused caches follow the same dependency checks: age is neither a waiting period nor deletion authority. Explicit owner-authorized retirement of obsolete evidence has its own scope; cache rules do not authorize deleting raw purchases or unique research results.

Netdisk transfers preserve original filenames and directory structure. Do not create externally stored opaque split `.part` wrappers or pack multiple inputs solely for transport. Use per-file identity locks while transfer processes run, with bounded upload concurrency and receiver verification. These are runtime coordination locks, not backup payloads. Exclude runtime locks from archived/transferred manifests and copies; never remove a lock still used by a process. Preserve dependency lockfiles such as uv.lock and requirements.lock and keep unknown-role files until identified. Provider-internal multipart requests may still produce one normal original-named cloud file. Meaningful dataset shards and unfinished atomic cache-construction directories are not obsolete transport wrappers; never delete them by a broad `part` name match.

Output input/derivative identity, coverage/support, actual versus pending tests, changed consumers and blockers. Data-only work needs no quote decomposition or economic-gain claim.

## Transfer and backup route

Before bulk data transfer, proactively discover the best feasible source-to-destination route within the authorized scope; do not wait for the owner to identify a better source or default to uploading from the workstation. Inventory compatible, verified existing copies on the destination, peer execution hosts, local storage, ORICO and Netdisk. Compare local reuse/copy, peer-to-peer private-network transfer, direct public-network SSH and Netdisk as applicable; prefer a nearby existing copy when measurements support it, without assuming any route is always best. Evaluate total time to verified availability, stability, resumability, setup overhead, egress cost, temporary disk needs and interference with active jobs. Count every transfer leg, including Netdisk upload plus download when needed, and avoid routing cloud-to-cloud payloads through the workstation when an authorized direct route is better. Use recent route-specific evidence or a bounded representative whole-file trial; never benchmark by duplicating the full batch. Briefly state the selected source, route and evidence, then execute without unnecessary confirmation within existing authority. Honor explicit route restrictions; if the chosen route fails, inspect alternatives and reassess instead of blindly retrying or merely reporting the first failure. Network/key changes still require existing authority: use narrowly scoped access and revoke temporary rules and credentials after transfer. Switch only after stopping the old writer safely; preserve sources and partial work, avoid concurrent writers, and verify received files rather than trusting successful client exit codes.

For NarrowGate transfers and backups, keep each existing data file whole and preserve its filename and directory structure. The owner does not want file splitting: do not introduce `.part` transport fragments, generic `part-000001.parquet` data shards, or packing of separate files merely for transport or convenience. Use descriptive market/symbol/data-type/date names for newly produced datasets. If a concrete format or tool limitation truly requires splitting, explain it and obtain approval first; do not assume a need based on file size. Provider-internal multipart upload is acceptable only when the stored result is one normal whole file. Existing shard names do not authorize deletion or blind renaming: identify contents and update consumers safely before any approved consolidation. Follow the checkout's data workflow for integrity checks and cleanup authority.

## Working-cache lifecycle

Choose prepare reuse versus regeneration by total cost to verified availability: transfer/copy, validation, staging and eventual cleanup versus generation, validation and archival, including CPU/RAM/disk pressure, failure risk and interference with running jobs. Prefer an already verified compatible copy on the destination. On the workstation, explicitly check the mounted ORICO archive before rebuilding; when reuse has lower total cost, copy and reuse that exact account/version instead of regenerating it. Do not inherit a cloud-transfer decision for the workstation. Oracle or other remote hosts may generate locally when this is cheaper than transfer, but first check compatible destination/peer copies and prove that generation preserves the required input/cache identity. Never change an expected hash or silently regenerate cross-platform features to make a cheaper route pass. Record the selected route and a brief evidence-based reason; do not benchmark by duplicating a full batch.

Archive reusable prepare on ORICO and Netdisk; replay from the execution host's local working disk, not directly from external storage. Stage only the account/version needed for current or near-term work within the disk budget, verify coverage, relative filenames and per-file sizes below, then bind it to that local directory. Reuse one compatible read-only market prepare across its consumers; account/order/inventory state remains independent. Do not copy again for every arm or prefill the whole calendar. Reserve space for active workers, outputs and the system; insufficient capacity means waiting, not lowering protection or changing active paths.

The lifecycle is required: retained archive -> bounded local staging -> accepted replay -> prompt working-copy removal. After the last actual consumer of an account/version completes and its economics/results are accepted, confirm the archive, process exit, recovery references and open files, then remove the disposable local prepare and retire its working-cache availability in the existing location index. Do not wait for the whole experiment to finish, disk exhaustion or another owner reminder. Queued-only copies may be evicted when the exact retained archive and the queued task's original restoration binding/path have been verified; otherwise retain them and state the missing restoration dependency. Never remove an active cache, the only accepted copy, or a cache whose consumer/recovery state is UNKNOWN. Keep the archives, original observations/timelines and result evidence.

Before launching a new executor or batch, verify that both staging and post-consumer cleanup have an actual responsible execution/maintenance path. Copy-from-archive support alone is not a complete lifecycle. If the frozen runner lacks cleanup, perform the authorized bounded cleanup through an existing maintenance path at completed-consumer boundaries; do not hot-edit it, restart healthy workers, create a new recurring automation without authorization, or claim that editing this skill installed automatic cleanup. Record completed cleanup or the concrete retention blocker in the owning experiment's existing receipt and synchronize location/file inventories without duplicating archives or inventing deleted scan counts.

Preserve reusable Final and development prepare datasets in both ORICO and the owner's Baidu Netdisk; task-local copies are working caches, not the only archive. Inventory existing local, ORICO, Shanghai, Japan and netdisk copies before transferring duplicates. For prepare deduplication and transfer acceptance, the owner chooses matching account/date coverage and cache version plus identical relative file lists and per-file byte sizes; do not recompute SHA checksums. This is size-based acceptance, not a claim of cryptographic equality, and does not alter execution-layer identity contracts. Preserve a single location/coverage inventory for reuse. Remove redundant local copies or obsolete transfer directories only after confirming retained copies, completed transfers and no active readers; never delete an only copy or an active Shanghai download source.

Automatically filter runtime lock files from every NarrowGate backup, transfer and archive, including prepare, input and result directories; do not ask again for each occurrence. This includes zero-byte locks and runtime locks containing a PID or owner record. Apply the same exclusion to source and destination inventories, deduplication and completeness checks, including when an older manifest lists those locks. Do not upload, download, recreate or retry missing runtime locks. Their absence is not missing data and must not block acceptance when data and required metadata match. During authorized archive cleanup, remove old runtime-lock copies only after confirming the location is inactive; do not archive those copies elsewhere. Filtering a backup does not authorize deleting locks from active working directories or disabling runtime locking. Identify files by role rather than blindly excluding every `*.lock`: preserve dependency lockfiles such as `requirements.lock`, `uv.lock`, `poetry.lock`, `Pipfile.lock`, `yarn.lock` and `Cargo.lock`, as well as required metadata. Keep unknown-role files until their purpose is established.
