## Linked coefficients and asymmetric offset: two control axes

This four-arm development comparison crossed linked risk coefficients with asymmetric offset. It studied the complete quote controller, not a newly trained predictor. The historical gamma label denotes coefficients varied together; current fields retain their actual consumer meanings rather than becoming one configuration alias. Its A/B/C/D arms are distinct from the threshold experiment's A00/A11/A10/A01 below.

On the same complete development account, the higher coefficient without offset reduced loss most, but increased peak inventory. Adding offset at the lower coefficient also reduced loss; adding the same offset at the higher coefficient reversed most of that improvement. All four arms remained negative. The axes therefore interact through the closed-loop path: individual improvements cannot simply be added, and one account cannot establish a universally beneficial offset or optimal coefficient. The later choice of D as an offline reference is a research configuration decision, not proof that D won this comparison. The first batch's failed risk non-inferiority conclusion remains intact.

## Existing-order update conditions: directional thresholds

These A00/A11/A10/A01 analysis arms are not the coefficient-axis A/B/C/D. The threshold controls when to update an existing order to the original target, not the target-price formula. Reducing it by five ticks does not move the target another five ticks. Outward means lowering BUY or raising SELL; inward is the opposite. Subsequent inventory divergence can naturally change later targets.

| Analysis arm | Ordinary outward ticks | Ordinary inward ticks | Reducing ticks |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A00 | 15 | 15 | 15 |
| A11 | 10 | 10 | 15 |
| A10 | 10 | 15 | 15 |
| A01 | 15 | 10 | 15 |

A00 reuses original B0; A11 is the already completed uniform threshold reduction, subsequently given an analysis alias without changing its identity or results. Only A10/A01 were added. Each arm covers the same 54 independent Final107 accounts, not one continuous 107-day account or randomized single-action interventions. A11 was nearly unchanged and slightly worse; A10 reduced loss but gains were concentrated, its paired median was negative and peak inventory increased; A01 was worse overall. Stable optimality was not established and no arm was promoted to B0. Private returns and configurations are not distributed.

The interaction is `I = P11 - P10 - P01 + P00`, with `P11 - P00 = (P10 - P00) + (P01 - P00) + I`; the two single-direction effects cannot simply be added. Equal seeds do not guarantee equal per-request samples after closed-loop divergence. Final had already been viewed, making this exploratory. The original directional batch did not load local quote-compute samples; this does not mean REST/order latency was zero, nor should it be pooled with the later compute-enabled scenario.

## Dynamic outward thresholds from target-quote variance rate

This study belongs to F01 because it compares a threshold control function, not a newly trained fill-value model. After target-publication observation and state-restoration checks, two complete development accounts compared dynamic D and fixed-scale S against reused, matched B0 results with local quote-compute time enabled. D changes only the ordinary outward threshold; inward, reducing, timing and other order rules remain unchanged.

The empirical function is `h = (12 * R0 * v_t)^(1/4)`, converted to integer price ticks. Each side's `v_t` is a variance-rate proxy from its own closed-loop target quotes, including inventory, prediction and risk feedback, not pure market variance. R0 has price-squared times seconds units and is not a measured adjustment cost. One R0 and S's typical fixed scale were determined from existing B0 target sequences before candidate returns were read. D/S share readiness and fallback rules, retaining the original threshold while unready, without clipping to 10/15 bounds.

Both D and S were worse than B0 in the two-account aggregate. D was slightly better than S overall, but the account-level direction was inconsistent; some improvements per unit of turnover did not produce better total net PnL. This bounded exploration supports neither adoption nor stable incremental value of the dynamic relation over a comparable fixed scale. No further R values were scanned, so it does not reject every possible scale. The same accounts supplied input diagnostics, reference scales and economic exploration; this is not independent validation.

Directional thresholds ask how far a target must move before updating; [F07 U0/U1](../f07_active_order_continuation/README.md) asks when to reevaluate targets. These are different control layers, and evidence for either does not automatically support the other.
